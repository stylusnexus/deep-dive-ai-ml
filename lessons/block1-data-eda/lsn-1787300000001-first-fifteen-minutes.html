The First Fifteen Minutes

A survey, not a clean-up — and the four things you must find before touching anything.

There's a strong pull, on opening a new dataset, to start fixing things. Resist it for fifteen minutes. The first pass is reconnaissance, and going in with a repair instinct means you fix symptoms before understanding the problem.

The four findings that change everything downstream

1. The target's balance
Before any metric
A 15% positive rate means 85% accuracy is free. Every score you read later is interpreted against that number.
2. Leaking features
Before any model
Any column that only exists because the outcome already happened. Catch it now or discover it in production.
3. Duplicates
Two kinds
Identical rows, and the same real thing recorded twice under different ids. The second kind is easy to miss and inflates your scores.
4. Missingness pattern
Not just the amount
Which columns, how much, and — the important part — whether the missing rows differ systematically from the rest.

Finding the leak

The Block 1 dataset carries a column called maintenance_ticket_raised. It looks like a legitimate operational feature. Cross-tabulate it against the target and every URGENT value corresponds to a failure — because the ticket is raised in response to the failure.

Train on it and you get a near-perfect model that cannot work in production, because at prediction time the ticket doesn't exist yet. The question that catches this is one line from your intake sheet: would I have this value, with this content, at the moment I need the prediction?

Why the survey pass finds it and later passes don'tOnce you're tuning a model, a feature that helps enormously feels like good news. In the survey pass you have no investment in the result, which makes suspicion cheap. This is the strongest argument for doing reconnaissance before modelling rather than alongside it.

Baseline before anything

Write down what "always guess the majority class" scores, before you build anything. In the Block 1 data that's 84.6%. A model reporting 86% accuracy is then immediately legible as a modest gain rather than an impressive one — and a model reporting 84% is legible as no gain at all.

What not to do in this pass

  • Don't impute. You don't yet know why values are missing, and filling before understanding destroys the signal you'd have used.
  • Don't drop columns for being messy. Messy and useless are different. The missingness flag in Block 1 turned out to be one of the better features.
  • Don't fit anything. Not a scaler, not an encoder. That comes after the split, for reasons the pipeline lesson covers.
  • Don't start engineering features. You'll build things you later throw away.
