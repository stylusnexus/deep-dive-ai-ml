Chain-of-Thought and Reasoning Tokens

Why letting a model think out loud works — and why its explanation isn't necessarily its reason.

Ask a model a multi-step question and it answers immediately, often wrongly. Ask it to work through the steps and accuracy improves markedly on the same question with the same model. That's chain-of-thought, and the mechanism behind it is more interesting than the trick.

Why thinking out loud helps

A model does a roughly fixed amount of computation per token generated. Demanding an immediate answer to a problem needing five steps allocates one token's worth of computation to five steps of work. Generating intermediate steps gives the model more forward passes, and — crucially — each step it writes becomes part of the context for the next, so it can build on its own partial results.

The reframing worth keepingChain-of-thought is not the model becoming smarter. It's the model being given room to compute. The generated tokens are working memory made visible — which is also why it helps far more on arithmetic, logic and multi-step planning than on recall, where there's nothing to work through.

The variants

Zero-shot CoT
"Think step by step"
One line, no examples, and a real improvement on reasoning tasks. Among the highest-value prompt changes available.
Few-shot CoT
Examples that show the working
Demonstrate the reasoning style you want, not just the answers.
Self-consistency
Sample several chains, take the majority
Costs several times more and reliably beats a single chain. Buying accuracy with tokens.
Reasoning models
Trained to reason before answering
The model produces an extended internal chain, sometimes hidden, before its visible answer. Reasoning effort becomes a parameter you set.

Reasoning tokens change the cost model

With reasoning models you pay for tokens you may never see. A short answer can sit on top of thousands of hidden reasoning tokens, all billed. Cost stops tracking visible output length and starts tracking problem difficulty — which is a different budgeting exercise, and one that surprises people the first time.

It also changes when to reach for what. Simple extraction, classification and formatting don't benefit from reasoning and just cost more slowly. The gains concentrate in genuinely multi-step problems — which is a routing decision, and exactly the kind of judgement escalation logic has to encode.

The part people get wrong

A chain-of-thought is not an explanation of how the model reached its answer. It's more text, generated by the same next-token process, that happens to look like reasoning. Models can produce a flawed chain and a correct answer, or a plausible chain that rationalises an answer they were always going to give. Research on unfaithful reasoning shows models omitting the cue that actually determined their output while presenting a tidy chain that doesn't mention it.

What this means for youTreat a chain as a debugging aid and a verification surface, not as an audit trail. It's genuinely useful — you can check the intermediate steps, spot where a calculation went wrong, and catch a wrong premise. But do not present it to a regulator, a customer, or a colleague as the reason the system decided something. It is what the model said about its reasoning, which is not the same object.

Where this reappears

Block 4's agents reason before choosing tools, and a visible chain is often the only way to debug why the wrong tool was called. Block 5 measures whether the chain actually supports the conclusion, which is a distinct question from whether the conclusion is right. And rationale distillation from the previous lesson trains a small model on these chains — one of the more effective ways to move reasoning capability downward.

Worth knowing

Chain-of-thought is not the model becoming smarter — it is the model being given room to compute.

A model does roughly fixed work per token. Demanding an immediate answer to a five-step problem allocates one token's worth of computation to five steps. Generating the steps buys more forward passes, and each step becomes context the next one can build on.

For exampleAsk for the answer to a multi-step arithmetic word problem and you get a wrong number instantly. Ask the same model to work through it and it gets it right — same weights, same prompt otherwise. Now ask it to think step by step about the capital of Portugal: no gain, because there were never any steps to take.
