Loops in Training: Flywheels and Model Collapse

How a model improves itself across generations — and the way that process eats itself.

The previous lesson looped at inference, leaving the weights untouched. This one loops around training: the model generates data, some of it is kept, the model is retrained, and round it goes. This is how a great deal of current capability was built, and it carries a failure mode with no equivalent at inference time.

The training loops that work

reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF)
Humans rank, model learns
The loop from the feedback lesson. Human judgement enters at ranking; the reward model then stands in for people at scale.
Rejection sampling / Self-Taught Reasoner (STaR)
Keep only what verified
Generate many attempts, keep the ones reaching a correct answer, fine-tune on those. The model bootstraps from its own successes — the filter is what makes it work.
Self-play
Compete against yourself
The AlphaGo-style loop. Needs a game with a definite winner, which is why it transfers to code and maths and not to essays.
Constitutional / reinforcement learning from AI feedback (RLAIF)
Model critiques itself against principles
A written constitution supplies the outside signal in place of a human rater, which is what stops it being pure self-reference.
Distillation flywheel
Big model teaches small
The teacher is the external signal. Covered in its own lesson.
The same rule, one level upEvery training loop above works for the reason inference loops work: something outside the model's own opinion decides what gets kept. A verifier in STaR. A human in RLHF. A game result in self-play. A written constitution in RLAIF. Remove the filter and you have a model training on its own unfiltered output — which is the next section.

Model collapse

Train a model on data generated by a previous model, repeatedly, without fresh real data or a filter, and quality degrades in a specific pattern. The tails of the distribution vanish first — rare events, unusual phrasings, minority cases — and then the centre narrows. Shumailov et al. demonstrated this across model families and published it in Nature in 2024.

The mechanism is not mysterious. Any sample under-represents the tails of the distribution it came from. Train on that sample and your model's distribution is slightly narrower. Sample from that, and it narrows again. Errors accumulate in one direction because sampling error is not symmetric in its effect on rare events — and there's nothing pulling the distribution back out.

Why this matters even if you never pretrain a modelThe public web is now substantially model-generated, and the effect applies to anything trained on scraped text. It also applies at your scale, in miniature: fine-tune on your own model's outputs across a few cycles without filtering and you'll narrow it toward its own habits. If you distil, keep the teacher's outputs filtered by something external — tests, human review, a verifier — rather than accepting them wholesale.

The other training-loop dangers

  • Reward hacking. The loop optimises the measure rather than the goal. Longer answers if raters prefer length; hedged answers if raters penalise being wrong.
  • Sycophancy. A specific, well-documented outcome of preference loops — agreement is rewarded, so agreement is learned.
  • Narrowing. Heavy preference tuning makes outputs safer and more uniform and less useful at the edges. A quieter cousin of collapse.
  • Evaluation contamination. If generated training data is drawn from the same pool as your eval set, your scores rise without capability rising. The benchmark version of leakage.

What keeps a training loop honest

A filter that isn't the model
Non-negotiable
Tests, execution results, human review, a verified answer key. Something with an opinion the model can't manufacture.
Fresh real data each round
Anchors the tails
Mixing genuine data with generated data substantially slows collapse. Purely synthetic loops are the dangerous case.
Held-out evaluation
Untouched by the loop
The loop must not be able to see the set that judges it, or it will optimise it.
Diversity monitoring
Watch the tails
Track output variety across generations, not just quality. Collapse shows up as narrowing before it shows up as being wrong.

Where this lands for you

You will not pretrain a foundation model. You will very plausibly fine-tune on data a model generated — that's the distillation recipe from Block 7, and it's a good recipe. The difference between the version that works and the version that quietly degrades is entirely the filter. Generate widely, keep only what passes something external, mix in real data, and evaluate on a set the loop never saw.

Worth knowing

Something outside the model's own opinion must decide what gets kept.

Training loops that work all have a filter: a verifier, a human rater, a game result, a written constitution. Remove the filter and you are training on your own unfiltered output, which narrows the distribution a little more each generation.

For exampleSTaR generates many attempts and keeps only those reaching a verified correct answer — self-training that improves. Fine-tuning repeatedly on whatever your model produced last time, with nothing checking it, loses the rare and unusual cases first and converges toward the model's own habits.
