The Map: AI, ML, Deep Learning, GenAI and Agents

Read this before anything else. Every term in the program has a place on one picture.

These five words get used as though they were alternatives to each other. They aren't — they nest. Most confusion in this field comes from treating a category and one of its subsets as if they were rivals, and almost every vendor pitch you will ever read depends on that confusion going unnoticed.

Reading the rings from the outside in

Artificial intelligence
The whole field
Any system doing something that looks intelligent. A chess engine from 1997 and a thermostat's scheduling logic both qualify. Broad enough that on its own the word carries very little information.
Machine learning
Learns from data
Rather than being given rules, it is given examples and infers the rules. Everything in Block 2 lives here — and it does not require a neural network.
Deep learning
Neural networks, many layers
Learns its own features rather than being handed them. Needs more data and more compute, and wins decisively on images, audio and language.
Generative AI
Produces content
Outputs text, images or audio instead of a label or a number. A spam classifier is ML but not generative; an image generator is both.
Agentic AI
An architecture, not a model
Sits outside the nesting deliberately. It is a loop that lets a model choose actions and use tools — you could build one around a small classical model, though in practice it's built around an LLM.

The distinction that actually matters day to day

Predictive versus generative. A predictive model answers a closed question — which category, what number, how likely. Its output can be checked against a known right answer, which makes evaluation straightforward and the statistics well-established.

A generative model produces open-ended content. There is usually no single right answer, which is why evaluating generative systems is a research problem rather than a formula, and why this program gives evaluation its own week.

Why the older material still mattersIt is tempting to skip Block 2's regression and decision trees to get to the LLM material. Don't. Overfitting, train/test splits, leakage, precision and recall, the bias–variance trade — these were all discovered in the predictive world and they apply unchanged to everything after it. Benchmark contamination is a test-set leak. Reward hacking is overfitting. The vocabulary transfers wholesale.

Where each block of this program sits

One honest caveat about the picture

Neat diagrams like the one above are simplifications, and this one has a visible seam: reinforcement learning doesn't sit tidily inside any ring — it's a third learning paradigm alongside supervised and unsupervised, and it shows up both in classical ML and in how LLMs are aligned. Treat the rings as a map for orientation rather than a taxonomy to defend.
