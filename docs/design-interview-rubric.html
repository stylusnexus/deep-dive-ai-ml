A scored mock interview for the questions AI system-design interviews keep asking. It tests whether you can say the course, under a clock, to another person. Quizzes test recall and the capstone tests building. This tests explanation.

Taught in Designing the Whole System and the lessons it points to.

How to run it

  • Two people. One asks, one answers, then swap. A single person can run it by recording themselves and scoring the recording the next day.
  • 45 minutes. Question 1 gets 12 minutes with a blank page. Questions 2 to 9 get 3 minutes each. The last 9 minutes are for scoring.
  • No notes, no screen. Draw by hand.
  • The interviewer may ask one follow-up per question. "How would you know?" is always allowed.

Scoring

Score each question 0, 1 or 2.

ScoreMeaning
0Names a tool or a buzzword with no reason, or avoids the question
1The right idea, with no failure named or no measurement named
2The right idea, the failure it prevents, and the number that would show it failed

Add three marks across the whole interview, 0 or 1 each:

  • Asked for the five numbers (corpus size and churn, query rate, latency budget, cost of a wrong answer, who may see what) before drawing, or stated assumptions aloud.
  • Named a failure of their own design before the interviewer did.
  • Said what they would build first and what they would leave out until measured.

Maximum 21. 17 or more: ready. 12 to 16: one weak block, re-read the lessons below. Under 12: redo the capstone failure drill and the Block 3 notebooks, then try again.

The questions and what a 2 contains

#QuestionA 2 answer includesLessons
1Design a production-ready RAG system.The five numbers first. Separate ingestion and query paths drawn. Each box with what it protects against: gateway (auth, limits), cache, query rewrite, hybrid retrieval with the access filter, rerank, context assembly with citations, generation under a deadline with a fallback ladder, a grounding check, traces. The four numbers it would report separately.building-a-rag-system, ranking-and-hybrid-retrieval, designing-the-whole-system
2When would you choose RAG over fine-tuning?Knowledge that changes, must be cited or must be access-controlled goes in retrieval. Fixed format, tone or a narrow skill goes in tuning, after prompting and retrieval were measured. Cites the failure: a tuned model can learn the rule and its blind spot.when-fine-tuning-is-wrong, building-the-dataset
3How would you reduce latency without hurting quality?Separates time to first token (prefill, grows with prompt) from tokens per second (decode). Fewer, better chunks; stable prefix; streaming; rerank a short list. Re-runs the eval set after each change.latency-and-caching, why-the-gpu-is-waiting
4What happens if your LLM provider goes down?Distinguishes down, throttled, slow, partial and degraded. Deadline plus circuit breaker. A tested fallback ladder ending at the retrieved passages or an honest error. Does not fail over on a 429. Names that the fallback needs its own evaluation and data agreement.when-the-provider-goes-down, managed-cloud-surfaces
5How do you evaluate an AI system?Reports index recall, retrieval quality, groundedness and correctness separately, on a private gold set with a non-completion column, and checks a difference is larger than noise.where-evaluation-lives, eval-data-you-can-trust, is-the-difference-real
6Where would you add caching?Exact-match first, then provider prompt caching with the stable prefix first, then a similarity cache only where a wrong match is cheap and never shared across tenants. Measures hit rate and wrong-hit rate.latency-and-caching
7How do you prevent hallucinations?Says they cannot be prevented, only made detectable: ground in retrieved text, require citations, check claims against sources, refuse when retrieval is empty. Measures groundedness and the false-refusal rate.hallucination-and-grounding, measuring-retrieval
8What would you monitor in production?Up and right as separate questions. Latency percentiles, error and fallback rates, cost per request, cache hit rate, plus a scheduled quality run on a fixed set. Says what wakes a person.after-it-ships, drift, telemetry-and-instrumentation
9How would you scale to millions of documents?Does the memory sum first. Compress and rerank, partition by tenant or source, shard only when needed and notes the slowest shard sets latency. Plans the re-index for a new embedding model. Measures recall against exact search.retrieval-at-scale, vector-search-algorithms

Drawing the complete architecture is question 1's figure, so it is not scored separately.

Failure patterns to listen for

  • A product name where a reason should be.
  • A cache or fallback with no mention of what it can get wrong.
  • "We would evaluate it" with no set, no number and no comparison.
  • Scaling answers that start with sharding and never mention how many vectors there are.
  • A design with no failure named until the interviewer finds one.

After the interview

For every question scored 0 or 1, write the missing sentence on the page and read the lesson in the last column. Re-run only those questions a week later.
