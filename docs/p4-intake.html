1. Is this even a machine learning problem?

Could a rule, a lookup table, a SQL query, or a human doing it twice a day solve this? If yes, say so and stop. Most "AI projects" die here and should.

2. What is the dumb baseline, and did you build it?

Predict the mean. Always guess the majority class. Return the most recent value. Keyword search instead of embeddings. Build it first. If your model can't beat it, you've learned something cheap.

3. What data exists?

How much, where from, who owns it, is it labeled, how good are the labels, and is it representative of what you'll see in production?

4. What decision does the output drive?

Not "insight." A decision. Who makes it, how often, and what do they do differently depending on the answer? If nothing changes, the project has no value.

5. What does "good enough" mean, as a number?

Decide this before you see any results, or you will rationalise whatever you get.

6. What does being wrong cost, and is it symmetric?

A false negative on fraud detection and a false positive are not the same event. Say which one hurts more and by roughly how much.

7. What happens when it fails?

Not if. What's the fallback path, who gets alerted, and does the system degrade safely or confidently produce garbage?

8. What does this cost at 100x volume?

Per-inference cost, latency budget, and what breaks first when traffic multiplies.

9. Who is accountable for the output?

Especially where the output reaches a customer, a regulator, or a clinical decision.

10. What would make you kill this?

Name the condition now, while you're still objective.
