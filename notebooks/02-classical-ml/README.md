Runs on `data/turbine_clean.csv`, produced by Block 1 notebook 02. Run that first.

| Notebook | What it does |
|---|---|
| `01-metrics-and-thresholds.ipynb` | Confusion matrix by hand, ROC and precision-recall side by side, AUC verified empirically, and a threshold chosen from costs |
| `02-model-ladder.ipynb` | Five models on identical folds, compared threshold-free, with permutation importance |

## What the outputs actually show

**The two curves disagree, and the disagreement is the point.** AUC 0.770 looks
respectable; average precision 0.465 against a 0.155 random baseline is far less
flattering. The false-positive *rate* stays small because there are many
negatives, while the false alarms pile up in absolute terms.

**The AUC interpretation is verified, not asserted.** The notebook draws 200,000
random positive/negative pairs and counts how often the positive scores higher:
0.7698 against sklearn's 0.7705.

**The cost curve has an interior minimum at 0.55**, not at the default 0.5 - and
an exercise shows that at a higher cost ratio the optimum moves to "inspect
everything", which is a correct answer rather than a bug.

**The model ladder is compared threshold-free.** Random forest looks poor on
recall at 0.5 (0.448) and is competitive on average precision (0.475). That gap is
a fact about the threshold, not the model - which is why the fair comparison uses
a threshold-free metric.

**The top three are indistinguishable.** Gap between the best two: 0.023.
Typical fold variation: 0.036. Not a demonstrated difference, so choose on
interpretability and operational cost instead.

## 03 · Neural networks

Builds the same network in Keras and raw PyTorch, and runs the Keras version
under both backends as a subprocess so you can see the code is genuinely portable.

Verified outputs: overfitting eight rows drives loss to 0.005 ("loop is wired");
the same code gives final loss 0.356212 on TensorFlow and 0.353911 on PyTorch;
and the network scores 0.462 average precision against the boosted tree's 0.457 -
indistinguishable, which is the lesson.

## 04 · Time series

Runs on `data/turbine_daily.csv` (generated by `scripts/make_timeseries.py`) -
two years of daily readings for 40 turbines, with trend, weekly seasonality, a
sensor recalibration at day 400, and a fortnight of telemetry loss.

Verified outputs: the random split scores 0.494 average precision and the
time-ordered split with a 7-day gap scores 0.343 - **optimistic by 0.151**, with
nothing about the model changed. Walk-forward folds range 0.029 to 0.441, which
is the honest uncertainty a single split would have hidden.

## 05 · MLP patterns, measured

Companion to the lesson `algorithms/mlp-patterns`. Needs PyTorch and NumPy, CPU only, a
few minutes; every dataset is synthetic and seeded, so the ground truth is known.

Verified outputs: one layer scores 0.525 on XOR and one hidden layer of eight scores 1.000.
At about 200 parameters a wide net averages 0.0250 error on a threefold-folded target and a
deep net 0.0389, so depth did not win. An autoencoder's error stops falling at a bottleneck
of 3, the true number of factors (0.0033 against a noise floor of 0.0025), where PCA needs 8.
A width-2 middle scores 0.8229 against a low-rank change alone and 0.0212 with a skip
connection. Two towers beat a joint model when affinity is a dot product (recall@10 0.865
against 0.819, 0.1 ms against about 25 ms) and lose when it depends on the pair (0.623
against 0.746). On a ring of eight clusters six GAN seeds reach 2, 8, 4, 2, 3 and 0 of them.
With 20 labels a frozen trunk and new head score 0.711 against 0.604 from scratch; with 1000
labels fine-tuning scores 0.929 against 0.888 frozen.
